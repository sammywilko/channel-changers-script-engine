## 1. Executive summary

**The opportunity is real, large, and growing — but the money in it is not where it first appears.**

Vertical micro-drama — 60–100 episodes of 60–120 seconds each, shot 9:16 for phones, monetised by paywalling the audience mid-cliffhanger — has gone from a Chinese domestic curiosity to a global category worth somewhere between $7.8bn and $14bn in 2026, depending on what you count `[ANALYST: Deloitte; Omdia]`. Outside China it is worth $3.6bn in 2026, forecast to $9.5bn by 2031 `[ANALYST: Media Partners Asia via Screen Daily]`.

**The UK is Europe's largest micro-drama audience** — 8.2m monthly actives in 2025, against Germany's 4.4m, and fifth globally by revenue in H1 2026 `[ANALYST: Omdia, May 2026]`. No British broadcaster is meaningfully serving that audience.

**But the platforms are not making much money, and neither would we by copying them.** ReelShort, the category leader, is forecast to turn its first profit in 2026 on $1.05bn of revenue — an EBITDA margin of roughly 6% `[ANALYST: Media Partners Asia via Variety, Aug 2026]`. The reason is user acquisition: marketing runs at five to nine times production cost, and 80–90% of individual titles never recoup `[PRESS: Campaign Asia; 36Kr]`. This is a portfolio business with mobile-gaming economics, not a television business.

**That asymmetry is the thesis.** The platforms carry the customer-acquisition risk. A production-side business does not. Channel Changers' opportunity is to be the supplier the platforms and broadcasters cannot easily replicate — English-language, British-originated, AI-accelerated, fast — while deliberately climbing out of the low-margin work-for-hire tier that most suppliers get trapped in.

**Four findings shape the plan:**

1. **UK tax relief does not apply.** AVEC fails on both the 20-minute-per-episode slot test and the £1m-per-hour spend floor; the film route fails HMRC's theatrical-intent test. Budget with zero credit (§7.3). This single finding removes roughly 25% from every naive UK financial model of this format.
2. **We cannot win on cost, so we must not try.** UK production runs ~£2,120 per screen-minute against ~£530 in China (§7.4). Competing on price against Zhengzhou is unwinnable. Competing on English-language originality, speed, and AI leverage is not.
3. **AI is production-ready for everything except emotional dialogue** — which is precisely what micro-drama is made of (§8.3). The workable 2026 architecture is hybrid, not fully generated. Anyone promising otherwise is selling something.
4. **There is a proven UK broadcaster precedent, and it is not Channel 4.** S4C commissioned a vertical drama, saw it nominated at two UK awards, and recommissioned at 8.5× scale (§10.1). That is the template for the co-production conversation.

**The recommended shape** is a three-engine business (§13): service work to fund the capability, brand-funded commissions for margin, and owned IP for equity value — with a broadcaster co-production as the credibility and IP-escalation play. Realistic three-year revenue is modelled in §11, and the honest conclusion there is that **service work alone does not build an investable company**; it buys the right to build one.

## 2. The market

### 2.1 Reconciling the numbers

The single most common error in this category is comparing figures that count different things. Anyone presenting a market-size slide without stating the denominator should be distrusted.

| Source | 2025 | 2026 | Out-year | What it actually counts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deloitte TMT Predictions 2026** `[ANALYST]` | $3.8bn | **$7.8bn** | — | In-app purchase revenue of dedicated micro-series apps; effectively excludes China domestic; excludes advertising |
| **Omdia** `[ANALYST]` | $11bn | **$14bn** (of which ~$3bn ex-China) | >$22bn by 2030 | Total revenue *including* China domestic — IAP, advertising and platform revenue |
| **Sensor Tower** `[ANALYST]` | $2.98bn FY25 (+115%) | ~$750m Q1 2026 (+20%) | — | App-store IAP only; excludes China Android; excludes advertising; **excludes web checkout** |
| **Media Partners Asia** `[ANALYST]` | $2.7bn ex-China | **$3.6bn ex-China** | $9.5bn by 2031 (21% CAGR) | Ex-China consumer revenue across app *and* web-checkout rails |
| **China domestic (duanju)** `[PRESS/DISCLOSED]` | RMB 67.79bn projected | — | >RMB 150bn by 2030 | Whole Chinese industry. RMB 50.5bn (~$7bn) in 2024 — the first year it exceeded China's box office |

**How they reconcile.** Omdia ≈ Deloitte + China domestic + advertising. Omdia's ex-China figure (~$3bn) sits between Sensor Tower's store-only run-rate and MPA's all-rails $3.6bn; the difference is web checkout, which operators use to avoid app-store commission. Deloitte's $7.8bn is the outlier — roughly 2× MPA for the same year, implying 105% growth that is not visible in Q1 2026 tracking.

**Recommended usage: quote MPA's $3.6bn ex-China as the base case and Deloitte's $7.8bn as the bull case. Never average them.**

Two figures circulating in the market should be actively rejected: a syndicated headline reporting Omdia at "$140bn by end of 2026" (a factor-of-ten error), and Holywater/Owl & Co's "$150bn vertical video economy," which measures the entire vertical-video advertising economy including Reels and TikTok — the same report puts dedicated micro-drama apps at $4bn `[VENDOR]`.

### 2.3 The growth curve is bending

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Global short-drama IAP `[ANALYST: Sensor Tower]` | ~$700m (~4× YoY) | ~$750m (**+20% YoY**) |
| Global downloads | ~370m | **850m (+140% YoY)** |
| Non-China Android IAP `[ANALYST: Naavik]` | $441m (+283%) | $530m (+20%); range-bound for several quarters |

**Downloads growing 140% against revenue growing 20% is the defining fact of 2026.** Naavik calls it the "volume vs. value paradox": acquisition is outrunning monetisation, and revenue per install is falling. Caveat in both directions — these figures exclude China Android *and* advertising revenue, toward which operators are actively shifting, so category revenue is understated by an unknown margin.

**Consolidation is underway.** The top three apps hold ~60% of non-China revenue; the top five (ReelShort 29%, DramaBox 21%, DramaWave 13%, NetShort 10%, GoodShort 6%) hold 79% `[ANALYST: MPA]`. Top five = 68.8% of revenue but only 28.8% of downloads `[ANALYST: Sensor Tower]`. Pocket FM closed its micro-drama app Pocket TV in 2026 citing competitive intensity `[PRESS]`. TikTok entered directly with PineDrama (US and Brazil, January 2026), already #3 by downloads in May `[PRESS: Tubefilter]`.

## 3. What is actually working right now

### 3.1 Genre: romance is saturated, horror is the opening

Every major app competes on the same four families — billionaire/CEO romance, werewolf and vampire fantasy, revenge, secret heir. These are folk-tale architectures (Cinderella, Monte Cristo, the returning king) retooled for a coin economy `[ANALYST: Vitrina; IntelligentHQ]`.

- Romance remains >70% of top-grossing titles, but **horror and suspense are reported growing roughly 2× faster**, and thrillers show ~40% higher retention among male viewers `[VENDOR: Filmustage — directional only]`.
- Male viewership has risen from ~10% to as high as 30% in some territories. Holywater reports its male MAU share moving from 1.1% to 30.3% across seven quarters `[VENDOR — operator-proprietary]`.
- **Psychological horror** is named the most interesting emerging genre of 2026. The format argument is sound: portrait orientation heightens claustrophobia and mobile viewing amplifies intimacy.
- **Comedy and slice-of-life are under-supplied** and adapt easily across age brackets `[PRESS: Kidscreen]`.
- **Deep-lore fantasy should be avoided** — you cannot establish stakes in 45 seconds `[PRESS: Kidscreen]`.

US-specific note: werewolf/fantasy leads the US chart while revenge shows the highest completion rates, because every episode delivers a payoff `[VENDOR: opendrama.ai]`.

### 3.2 AI content is already a plurality in China

- In January 2026 a new AI micro-drama went live on a Chinese platform roughly **every 90 seconds**; ~50,000 AI-native titles were added to Douyin in March `[ANALYST/PRESS, aggregated]`.
- **AI-generated share of China's top charts rose from ~7% to ~40% within a year** `[ANALYST: Vitrina]`.
- Justine Moore (Partner, a16z) has published a market map explicitly splitting distribution into conventional and **AI-native** (Vigloo, TrueShort, Mochi, Shortly, DashReels, PopshortAI, My Muse, VibeShort) `[SOCIAL: @venturetwins]`.
- Vigloo (South Korea) released *Met a Savior in Hell* and *Seoul: 2053*, billed as the first fully AI-visual vertical dramas `[PRESS: Forbes, Nov 2025]`.

**The most important single data point for an AI-native studio:** Holywater reports payer conversion of **23.6% on its AI-generated My Muse against 23.9% on live-action My Drama** `[VENDOR — self-reported, platform-specific]`. Near-parity. If that holds at other operators, the cost advantage of AI production flows almost entirely to margin. It should be stress-tested before capital is committed against it.

**The countervailing evidence is real too.** YouTube's CEO named "AI slop" in a January 2026 letter and the platform removed 16 channels with 35m subscribers `[UNCLEAR — secondary aggregator]`. Chinese AI-actor likeness controversies have drawn public criticism `[PRESS: Sixth Tone]`. Marginal content cost approaching zero destroys scarcity value even as it improves gross margin.

### 3.3 Brands are entering, and this is under-priced

- **Crocs × SuperOrdinary — *Déjà Shoe***: a 7-part shoppable TikTok Shop series built in under four weeks `[PRESS/SOCIAL]`.
- **General Mills' Gushers** became the first brand to sponsor *Screen Time* — a TikTok-backed thriller from Issa Rae's HOORAE Media `[PRESS: Variety]`.
- **JCPenney** built a series as natively shoppable `[PRESS: Marketing Dive]`.
- **TikTok has launched a dedicated branded mini-drama ad tool** `[PRESS: Social Media Today]`.
- **Fox** has committed to 200+ vertical series through 2026 `[PRESS: Deadline]`.
- Variety covered micro-drama as a Cannes Lions 2026 story: *"The Micro Pitch: Why Brands Are Flocking to Microseries."*

**No disclosed fee exists for any of these deals.** That is a genuine gap (§14) — but it is also an opportunity, because it means the market has no public price anchor and a well-positioned studio can set its own.

### 3.4 The contrarian case, stated fairly

An investor will find these. Better that they come from us:

- **VideoWeek, February 2026:** *"There'll Definitely be a Backlash"* — micro-dramas come to Europe as studios bet on short-form.
- **Entertainment Strategy Guy, January 2026:** *"Micro-Dramas: Bubble, Goldrush, Fraud or Genuine Opportunity?"* The fraud framing is unusual and worth reading in full.

## 4. The format: what a micro-drama actually is

Full craft detail is in the companion document, **[SCRIPT-PLAYBOOK.md](./SCRIPT-PLAYBOOK.md)**. The commercial essentials:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Episodes per season | 60–100 (ReelShort commonly 80–90) |
| Episode runtime | 60–180 seconds; modal ~90s |
| Total runtime | ~90–150 minutes — a feature film, sliced |
| Script length | 400–600 words per episode |
| Shoot schedule | Full season in 8–10 days |
| Central characters | 2–3 maximum |
| Free episodes before paywall | 5–15, varies by title and A/B test |

**The unit of storytelling is the tap, not the episode.** Traditional television asks what happens in act two. Micro-drama asks what unresolved consequence makes someone tap *next*. Every episode ends mid-tension; the purchase decision happens in the final three seconds.

The Chinese industry's own vocabulary is more precise than anything in English trade press, and adopting it is a genuine competitive advantage:

- **黄金3秒** (*golden three seconds*) — the stopping threshold. Conflict, suspense or visual shock. Pick one; don't blend.
- **卡点 / 冲突点 / 反转点** (*cut-point / conflict-point / reversal-point*) — the working beat sheet.
- **爽点** (*shuǎng* — the satisfaction discharge) and **打脸** (*face-slap*) — the payoff mechanics.
- **情绪价值** (*emotional value*) — the governing design principle. Micro-drama runs on emotional suspense, not plot suspense.
- **一卡 / 二卡** (*first wall / second wall*) — paywall positions, which must be marked explicitly in the episode outline at development stage.

That last point is the one most Western producers miss: **the paywall is a script decision made at outline, not a commercial decision made after delivery.**

## 5. The money: how micro-drama monetises

### 5.1 The revenue stack

**Coin/unlock IAP.** First 5–15 episodes free, paywall landing mid-cliffhanger, then each episode is a discrete purchase at 50–200 coins (~$0.10–0.20). Coin ladders run $0.99 / $4.99 / $9.99 / $19.99 / $49.99 / $99.99. Completing one ~100-episode series costs $20–50 `[VENDOR]`. Payer conversion 3–8%; monthly ARPPU $15–35; blended ARPU $0.50–2.50; **top 10% of payers generate 40–60% of revenue** `[VENDOR]`.

**Subscription — bigger than commonly assumed.** MPA reports that consumer payments and subscriptions are 85–90% of ReelShort revenue, and **subscriptions already account for 60–70% of viewer spend** `[ANALYST: MPA via Variety]`. The popular "coins are everything" framing is wrong at the market leader. ReelShort VIP is $19.99/week or $199/year; DramaBox $5.99/week or $49.99/year `[VENDOR]`.

**Advertising.** Rewarded video at episode-end, at the coin-or-ad decision point. Tier 1 eCPM $15–40 `[VENDOR]`. Ads monetise the 92–97% who never pay.

### 5.2 The app-store bypass — and the threat to it

This is the live margin story:

- **30 April 2025:** the US court found Apple wilfully violated the 2021 anti-steering injunction. US-storefront apps may link out to external checkout at **zero Apple commission** `[PRESS]`.
- **ReelShort's route to profitability runs through its own web store**, bypassing commission of up to 30%. MPA states the web-store billing shift plus the subscription mix shift together account for **close to half of projected margin expansion** `[ANALYST: MPA via Variety/Deadline/TechTimes, 6 Aug 2026]`.
- **The EU does not replicate this.** External-purchase links there carry Apple fees of 12–20% plus payment processing `[VENDOR]`.
- **⚠️ On 13–14 August 2026 — two days before this report — Apple proposed charging up to 15% on external-link purchases** `[PRESS: TechCrunch; MacRumors]`. If granted, this removes a large slice of the margin the entire sector has engineered into its payment stack.

**Measurement consequence:** Sensor Tower tracks app-store IAP only and excludes web payments. As the web shift accelerates, every published Sensor Tower revenue figure increasingly *understates* true consumer spend. Do not compare an MPA total to a Sensor Tower total.

At US RPD of $4.70, that break-even implies ~390,000 US downloads converting at sector norms — achievable only for top-decile titles.

**This is why Western independents overwhelmingly take the buyout.** And it is why self-distribution is not a credible year-one strategy for a UK company.

### 5.4 What YouTube is actually worth

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts ad RPM | **$0.02–0.07 per 1,000 views** `[VENDOR, multiple concurring]` |
| **Value of 1 million Shorts views** | **$20–70** |

### 6.4 Ranked routes for Channel Changers

1. **Work-for-hire supply to tier-2 apps** (NetShort, My Drama, GoodShort, ShortMax, FlexTV). Cost-plus, cash-positive per title, no marketing risk, no audience required. You lose IP. **The only route that reliably pays in year one.** Enter with a completed 3–5 episode sample season, not a deck.
2. **RoseBerry / Epis and its UK supplier network.** Already commissioning UK originals and repurposing BBC/C4 library using AI workflows — a direct match for an AI-capable UK indie. **Warmest realistic door.**
3. **Brand-funded vertical drama.** Fee-for-service, uses the scripting strength, sidesteps RPM economics entirely. **Underrated and under-priced.**
4. **Non-exclusive licensing of self-financed AI-produced titles** via aggregators. Low fees per deal, stackable across territories; only defensible if per-title cost is genuinely in the AI range.
5. **TattleTV and UK domestic apps.** Small money, high strategic value — local credits and a reference case.
6. **Broadcaster co-production** (§10). Slowest, highest credibility, best IP terms.
7. **Direct submission to ReelShort/DramaBox.** Attempt it; expect low conversion.
8. **YouTube/TikTok as primary distribution.** Funnel only, never the business.
9. **Own white-label app. Do not.** The technology is cheap (Uscreen $149–549/month; Vimeo OTT from $1/subscriber + 10%). The ~9×-of-production user-acquisition spend is the barrier. Building the app without a UA war chest reproduces the cost and none of the advantage.

## 7. Production: what it costs and where to shoot

### 7.1 The Chinese model — understand the mechanism, not just the price

100 episodes in 7 days, crews of 60–90 for premium content, 10–15 script pages per shooting day `[ANALYST: Vitrina; Filmustage]`. A 100-episode series in Zhengzhou's Jinshui District costs **RMB 300,000–800,000 (~£43k–£116k)** with a 3–6 month return cycle `[PRESS: Xinhua/Qiushi, Apr 2026]`. Average modern 65-episode series: RMB 500,000–600,000; costume/period RMB 800,000–1.2m.

**The speed does not come from a magic crew.** It comes from co-located standing sets that eliminate company moves and lighting resets — the Dazhi Film & TV Base has 50+ standing sets in ~10,000m², serving 2,600+ production teams since April 2025. Banquet hall, mansion, hospital, subway platform, office and bedroom sit side by side, so units move between "locations" in minutes. Scripts are written *to* those fixed sets. Coverage is simplified for 9:16 — fewer angles, tighter blocking, minimal wides. These clusters are called *shudian* — "vertical Hengdian."

**Cost per screen minute: ~RMB 5,100 (~£530)** `[MODELLED from Xinhua figures]`.

### 7.2 The US model and SAG-AFTRA

LA vertical drama runs **$150,000–250,000 for a 7–10 day shoot producing 50–70+ episodes**; 8–10 shoot days at 15–20 script pages/day, 30+ crew `[PRACTITIONER: Real Reel]`. Los Angeles is considering a $5m support programme specifically for vertical micro-drama `[PRESS: Sheppard Mullin, Jan 2026]`.

**The SAG-AFTRA Verticals Agreement** — a promulgated New Media contract signed **13 October 2025** `[DISCLOSED: sagaftra.org]`:

| Term | Detail |
|---|---|
| Scope | Serialised micro-dramas, 9:16, mobile-first, **budget under $300,000** |
| Lead performer minimum | **$250 / 8 hours**; $468.75 / 12 hours — roughly 20% of the film/TV daily minimum |
| Other performers | $164/day |
| Pension & Health | **Full TV Agreement rate — 22% from 1 July 2026. No discount, no cap** |
| Residuals | **None** for new-media exhibition if original and budgeted below **$25,000 per minute** |
| Performer flexibility | Non-exclusive during photography; 36 hours' notice of conflicting engagements |

The $25,000/minute residual threshold is generous: an 80 × 90s series is 120 minutes, implying a $3m ceiling, far above any real budget. So a compliant US shoot at ≤$300k pays no new-media residuals. The real costs are the 22% P&H and the $300k budget ceiling.

> **⚠️ Unresolved and material:** one source indicates the agreement **auto-sunsets on 30 June 2026 unless extended**, and no source confirms whether it was. **This must be verified directly with SAG-AFTRA before any US-cast plan is costed.**

### 7.3 UK tax relief — the answer is no

This is the most consequential UK finding in the report, and it is negative.

**AVEC via the high-end TV route fails on two independent tests:**

| Requirement | Threshold | Micro-drama position |
|---|---|---|
| Slot length | **>20 minutes, applied episode-by-episode** | A 90-second episode fails outright. The old 30-minute test allowed aggregation of short episodes; government explicitly moved to per-episode application to close that. **The vertical loophole was deliberately shut.** |
| Core expenditure | **≥£1m per hour of slot length** | 80 × 90s = 2.0 hours ⇒ **£2m minimum spend**. A realistic UK build is £130k–£255k. Fails by ~10× |
| Programme type | Drama/comedy/documentary | Passes |
| UK core spend | ≥10% | Passes |
| Cultural test | 18/35 points | Passable |

`[DISCLOSED: BFI; British Film Commission; gov.uk policy paper]`

**AVEC via the film route fails theatrical intent.** There is no statutory minimum length for a film, and short films do claim relief `[PRACTITIONER: Stephen Follows]`. But the film must be *intended for theatrical release* — and HMRC accepts 5% of total estimated income as "significant," with stated indicators including a financial plan predicated on theatrical release and **a format suitable for theatrical showing** `[DISCLOSED: HMRC manual FPC40020]`. A 9:16 in-app series fails the format test and the 5% income test simultaneously.

**Independent Film Tax Credit is not a route** — 53% on up to £15m qualifying spend, but requires theatrical intent and is **expressly unavailable for TV programmes**.

**Verdict: budget UK vertical micro-drama with zero tax credit.** The only conceivable route is a genuine, provable theatrical strategy for a feature-length horizontal cut with the vertical series as a secondary window — an aggressive position requiring BFI Certification Unit and HMRC pre-clearance.

> **Not yet checked:** Screen Scotland, Ffilm Cymru, Northern Ireland Screen and Creative England regional funds. Given AVEC is closed, these are the likeliest source of UK public money and should be the first follow-up. Note that Creative Wales, North East Screen, NI Screen and Screen Scotland *did* co-finance the BBC's *Long Story Short* short-form slate (§10.3) — the template exists.

### 8.1 The strategic lesson first

### 8.3 The two things that are still broken

**Character consistency is not solved.** Every "95%" figure is a vendor benchmark measured on 10-second clips — within a shot, not across 100 episodes. Nobody publishes cross-episode consistency data, which is itself a warning. Character drift is documented across every major model. What genuinely helps, in order of leverage:

1. **Multi-image reference anchoring** — Kling V3 Omni accepts up to 7 reference images per generation. **This maps directly onto the existing Gen Suite `shared_assets` registry**: rows should hold a 5–7 image reference *set* per character (neutral pose, even lighting, clean background, multiple angles), not a single hero image.
2. **Reference quality discipline** — low-res, backlit or busy references measurably weaken identity anchoring.
3. **Character LoRAs** — 15–50 images, 1,000–3,000 training steps. The only route producing an asset *you own* rather than a per-call upload. Relevant for an 80-episode commitment.
4. **First-frame/last-frame locking** — turns identity into an image problem, then constrains motion between fixed endpoints.

**Emotional dialogue does not work yet.** Asked directly whether a 90-second two-hander can be generated without looking uncanny in August 2026, the sourced answer is no — not reliably, not at broadcast quality, not without heavy human post. The critical detail: **AI dialogue quality degrades as emotional intensity rises.** Neutral, news-style delivery works; betrayal, confrontation and revelation do not. Micro-drama is built entirely on the latter.

**The defensible 2026 architecture is therefore hybrid:**
- Human performance capture (Runway Act-Two class) or human-recorded VO plus dedicated post lip-sync for dialogue.
- Native-audio generation reserved for low-intensity connective scenes.
- Scripts structured to keep peak-emotion beats in reaction shots, over-shoulders and non-verbal moments — which is also just good vertical craft (§4).

### 8.4 Generation cost floor

`[MODELLED]` — 80 episodes × 90s = 120 finished minutes; 5s average usable shot ⇒ 18 shots/episode; **keeper rate 1 in 5** (assumption).

| Model | Per episode | 80 episodes | Per finished minute |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Veo 3.1 standard ($0.40) | $180 | $14,400 | $120 |
| Veo 3.1 Fast ($0.15) | $67.50 | $5,400 | $45 |
| MiniMax H3 ($0.13) | $58.50 | $4,680 | $39 |
| Runway Gen-4.5 ($0.12) | $54 | $4,320 | $36 |
| **Kling 3.0 ($0.10)** | **$45** | **$3,600** | **$30** |

**The keeper rate dominates model choice.** At 1-in-10 every figure doubles; at 1-in-3 it drops ~40%. The price spread between Kling and Veo standard is 4×; the plausible keeper-rate range is 3×. **Shot-acceptance rate is a bigger cost lever than model selection — instrument it from episode one.**

Critically, **this is an inference floor, not a budget.** It excludes voice, lip-sync, prompt engineering, reference-set curation, shot QA, edit, sound, grade and music — the human lines that actually determine whether "AI velocity" beats live action. A documented AI-assisted 10-episode series came in at **$1,000/episode all-in** with a 3-person crew over 3 days `[VENDOR case study]`, which implies ~$80k for 80 episodes and is the most useful real-world anchor found.

### 10.1 The precedent that makes the argument: S4C

**S4C is the only UK public service broadcaster found to have commissioned vertical drama — and it has done so twice, from the same indie.**

- ***Yr Alwad* (The Call)** — S4C's first vertical drama, for its youth brand Hansh, released natively **on TikTok**. Seven episodes, produced by **Mojo Productions**. Subject: homophobic bullying and the dangers facing young people on nights out `[TRADE: Televisual; Nation.Cymru]`.
- **Validated by the UK awards circuit** — nominated for **Best Vertical Series** at the National Film Awards and **Best Short-Form Scripted** at the Broadcast Digital Awards. This de-risks the format for a commissioner worried about prestige.
- ***Signal*** — commissioned **July 2026**. **60 episodes**, vertical, for Hansh/TikTok, Mojo Productions again. A romance between a boy from Cardiff and a girl from rural Wales, shooting at the Royal Welsh Showground `[PRESS: Televisual; Advanced Television; Señal News; Broadband TV News, 23 Jul 2026]`.
- **Explicit talent-development framing** — four new writers (Brynach Higginson, Mari Izzard, Rhiannon Williams, Alexander Williams), led by Gwenno Hughes.

**Why this is load-bearing:** it demonstrates the complete escalation arc a commissioner needs to see — pilot (7 episodes) → awards validation → recommission at 8.5× scale → same indie retained → talent-pipeline justification → CCO tying it to corporate strategy.

**The pitch is: "the S4C model, in English, at network scale."**

### 10.2 Channel 4 — the tariff numbers, with a caveat

Channel 4's `Fast Forward` strategy commits it to being a digital-first public service streamer by 2030, with investment shifting toward *"programming that drive[s] streaming growth... while targeting younger viewers on platforms including YouTube"* `[DISCLOSED: Channel 4 press]`. It launched *A Comedy Thing by Channel 4* on YouTube in July 2025 — long-form weekly with cutdowns to TikTok and Instagram.

**Published tariffs** `[DISCLOSED: Channel 4 4Producers]`:
- Digital original commissioning: **£1,000–£3,000 per minute** ⇒ **£60k–£180k per finished hour**
- Short-form drama brief: three-part series, 7–15 minutes per episode, **budget under £270k**

⚠️ **These two figures are internally inconsistent** — 3 × 15min = 45 minutes at £270k is £6k/minute, double the top of the stated digital tariff. Most likely scripted carries a premium. **Verify on 4Producers before quoting in a pitch.**

That is precisely where the IP negotiation lives, and it has not yet been read. It is the highest-value single follow-up in this section.

⚠️ **A claim that "Channel 4 is commissioning series specifically for vertical format" appears in AI-generated SEO content with no trade or C4 source. Treat as false pending confirmation. Do not put it in a deck.**

### 10.3 BBC — short-form yes, vertical no

***Long Story Short*** — a BBC Writers initiative: seven original short dramas across all four nations, launched 1 February 2026 on iPlayer and BBC Three `[PRESS: Northern Ireland Screen; Televisual]`. Each film produced by an independent supported by the **BBC's Small Indie Fund**, pairing new writing and directing voices with local indies.

### 10.4 ITV and Sky — approach the right entity

**Sky agreed a £1.6bn acquisition of ITV Media & Entertainment** (~6 July 2026), covering ITV1/2/3/4 and **ITVX**. **ITV Studios is excluded** and continues independently, supported by a new **£2.1bn five-year content supply agreement with Sky**. **Tactical read `[INFERENCE]`: ITVX and Sky are the worst near-term co-production targets** — 12–18 months of CMA process, £200m of cost-out, an unresolved content-supply relationship. **ITV Studios is the opposite**: now a standalone content business hunting growth and distribution differentiation, and Deadline has publicly named it as absent from a $22bn category. *"There are no obvious examples"* of Fremantle, ITV Studios, All3Media International or Banijay Rights packaging vertical video for platforms like ReelShort or DramaBox; a senior distribution source told Deadline **"This is a platform play"** `[TRADE: Deadline, 22 Feb 2026]`.

That is a named, public gap in a major British distributor's slate. It is pitchable.

### 10.5 Europe: the copyable template

**Banijay Productions Germany** unveiled its first micro-drama at Series Mania, Lille (March 2026): 28 episodes, cast of **eight German creators with 33m+ combined followers**, EP Imke Runde `[PRESS: Banijay; Broadcast; C21]`.

`[INFERENCE]` **Note the model: creator-led casting as the audience-acquisition mechanism, not star talent.** The 33m followers *are* the distribution; the format is the wrapper. For a UK indie with no audience, this is the most directly copyable European template — and it converts the biggest weakness (no owned audience) into a casting decision.

**Correction to a common misreading:** the Netflix–TF1 distribution pact beginning mid-2026 is **TF1 content on Netflix. It is not a micro-drama deal.** Do not misrepresent it.

### 10.6 The commissioner argument, assembled

`[INFERENCE — reasoning built on the sourced evidence above]`

1. **Youth reach where the PSB deficit is worst.** S4C put vertical *on TikTok itself* rather than on its own player. C4's *Fast Forward* and its YouTube channel show the same logic already accepted internally. Vertical is the cheapest available test of that thesis.
2. **IP funnel.** The BBC already treats short-form as pilots. S4C's 7 → 60 episode escalation is live UK proof that a vertical pilot can earn a scale order.
3. **Talent development at low risk.** Every UK short-form precedent found is justified publicly on talent-pipeline grounds. **Pitch a vertical slate as a writers' and directors' pipeline with an audience attached** — this unlocks talent budgets and screen-agency co-finance, not just content budgets.
4. **Cost per hour.** At C4's £1–3k/minute digital tariff, a finished hour is £60k–£180k, against high-end drama at multiples of that. Episode count also generates far more ad inventory per pound of content spend.
5. **Awards de-risking.** Both the Broadcast Digital Awards and National Film Awards now have categories a vertical series can win — and *Yr Alwad* was nominated in both.
6. **The Europe-shaped hole.** Omdia has China at 83% of 2025 revenue and the US at 9%, leaving ~8% for the entire rest of the world. **A UK broadcaster is not late to this market; it is early to its own.**
7. **The distributor layer does not exist.** Deadline's structural point, quotable: Chinese micro-drama services buy daily, direct from trusted vertical suppliers, which *"subverts and excludes the traditional TV distribution model."* A broadcaster that wants in must commission directly — which is the wedge for an indie.

## 11. The financial case

### 11.1 What kind of business this can be

Three revenue engines, with materially different risk and margin. The honest position on each is stated rather than smoothed.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Service / work-for-hire** | Platform commissions, cost-plus | 10–15% producer fee | Very low — no UA, no audience | `[VENDOR]` fee band; consistent across sources |

**Upside** — one owned title breaks out. On the §5.3 model, a hit at $5m gross billings throws off ~$0.9m of contribution against the same cost base. **One breakout title in three years changes the entire picture.** That is the option value being bought, and it is why the owned-IP engine matters even though it looks marginal in the base case.

### 11.4 What an investor is actually buying

Stated plainly: **not a content library, and not a platform.** They are buying:

1. **A production capability with a genuine cost advantage** — AI-assisted output at ~£650/episode against £1,600–3,200 for UK live-action, wrapped in a studio that already has the scripting and asset-consistency tooling built.
2. **Option value on owned IP** in a category where a single breakout returns more than three years of service revenue.
3. **A position in a market growing 21% CAGR ex-China** `[ANALYST: MPA]` where the UK is Europe's largest audience and no domestic broadcaster is serving it.
4. **Arms-dealer positioning.** ReelShort's ~6% EBITDA margin on $1.05bn is a CAC story. The supplier does not carry CAC.

**What they are not buying: a business that beats China on price.** That should be said out loud in the first meeting, because anyone who has done the work will ask.
